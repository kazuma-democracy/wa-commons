# WA Commons Manifesto

## Peace should not depend on goodwill alone

Many systems for peace, democracy, rights, and coexistence depend on people continuing to care, donate time, remember promises, investigate obscure records, and absorb personal costs.

People get tired. Institutions change. News cycles move on. Small failures accumulate.

AI agents introduce a different capability: software can keep watching, remembering, checking, matching, and following up long after human attention has moved elsewhere.

WA Commons exists to ask what happens if that persistence is used as public infrastructure.

## We are not building a machine that defines peace

No model should be granted authority to decide one universal political morality.

WA Commons instead separates:

- **values**, chosen by people and communities;
- **evidence**, which should be inspectable;
- **rules**, which should be explicit;
- **actions**, which should be permissioned and bounded;
- **results**, which should be measured and published.

Disagreement is expected. Contestability is a feature.

## Make the peaceful choice useful

Moral appeals matter, but systems that require sacrifice from every participant are difficult to scale.

Our preferred design pattern is different:

> A person uses a product because it saves time, improves a decision, reduces cost, manages risk, or creates opportunity. The architecture then gives a small structural advantage to choices consistent with that person's peace-related values.

Millions of tiny choices can become an economic signal.

## Weak agents, relentless agents

WA Commons favors agents with small permissions and long memories over agents with sweeping authority.

They should not secretly persuade people, punish opponents, impersonate citizens, or make irreversible high-impact decisions on their own.

They should be able to do boring things forever:

- re-check evidence;
- remember commitments;
- detect changes;
- reopen unresolved tasks;
- search for mutually beneficial alternatives;
- prepare an appeal;
- ask the same unanswered question again;
- record what happened.

## Open by default

We want the infrastructure to be inspectable, forkable, auditable, and reusable internationally.

We will prefer existing open-source projects and open standards over rebuilding solved components.

When we invent something genuinely new, we should be able to explain exactly what was missing from the existing ecosystem.

## From Japan to the world

“WA” is 和, 輪, 環, and 話: peace, connection, circulation, and dialogue.

Our ambition is not to export a Japanese political doctrine.

It is to export **peace technology**: transparent mechanisms that other communities can adapt to their own values and institutions.
