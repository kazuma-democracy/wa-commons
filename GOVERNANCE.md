# WA Commons Governance — Bootstrap Draft

This is intentionally a bootstrap model and should be replaced through an open governance process as the contributor base grows.

## 1. Mission is harder to change than implementation

The core mission is to develop voluntary, auditable, peace-positive public technology that provides direct utility to its users.

Technical architecture, scoring methods, data sources, and experiments are expected to change frequently.

## 2. No single moral score

The core infrastructure must not require one mandatory political or ethical ranking. Users and downstream communities should be able to define policy profiles while sharing a common evidence layer.

## 3. Evidence corrections

Named-entity classifications must have a documented correction/challenge path. Material disputes remain visible rather than being silently overwritten.

## 4. High-impact permissions

Autonomous execution involving real money, legal filings, political communication, physical systems, or other consequential actions requires a specific governance decision and safeguards. Research-mode automation does not automatically grant execution authority.

## 5. Transparency

Material decisions should be recorded in public decision logs including:

- decision;
- alternatives considered;
- evidence;
- expected benefit;
- known risks;
- reversibility;
- review date when appropriate.

## 6. Conflicts of interest

Contributors involved in classifications or standards should disclose material conflicts relevant to that decision.
